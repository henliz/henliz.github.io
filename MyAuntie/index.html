The Product

What is My Auntie?

New parents don't quit because they're unmotivated. They quit because the system makes help feel like homework. In the fourth trimester, energy and attention are the scarcest resources—precisely when most "solutions" demand the most: download an app, create an account, complete a 20-question intake, then wait.

My Auntie reframes support around the reality of 3 a.m.: a crying baby, 4% battery, and a brain made of mashed potatoes. No apps, no dashboards—just a warm voice or a short text that meets you where you are, offers one gentle next step, and (when needed) routes you to real care without judgment.

36-hour sprint goal: ship a live, phone-first, safety-aware companion that feels like calling a trusted auntie—tiny, kind, and doable. We launched SMS + voice, intent triage (Comfort · Resource · Escalate), privacy-first memory, and deterministic resource lookup—end-to-end and demo-ready.

The Problem

Nobody should feel alone at 3 a.m.

Postpartum depression and anxiety affect ~1 in 7 women; many never receive timely care. Stigma ("I should be happy"), sleep fragmentation, and logistical friction push parents away from support at the exact moment they need it.

The dominant pattern in digital mental health—apps that shift the burden back to the parent (log in, track, remember)—is backwards at 3 a.m. We needed a low-friction, high-comfort channel that lowers cognitive load, is safe by design, and escalates appropriately.

Reframe: don't build another mental-health app. Build what new parents actually use at 3 a.m.: the phone. A kind voice. A short text. A connector that knows when to comfort, when to send resources, and when to escalate.

You don't want an app. You want your Auntie. Information-only this weekend; deterministic escalation; short-lived context for safety.
The Design Framing

At 3 a.m., a new parent asks: "Is this normal?"

They don't need a portal, they need a person. Real aunties aren't always awake, and advice can be dated or risky. My Auntie keeps the warmth and removes the guesswork: zero setup, human-sounding support, and vetted resources when needed.

Two design constraints guided everything:

1. Cognitive load must be near zero. One-breath messages, one next step.

2. Safety must be explicit, not implied. Intent classification and clear red-flag routes to urgent care—without alarmism.

This created an unusual design challenge: how do you make technology feel like kin, not like software? Unlike an app with buttons and charts, Auntie had to carry her identity entirely in voice and text.
Auntie couldn't just be helpful—she had to be safe. The line between comfort and harm is razor-thin in postpartum care.
We aimed for emotional specificity: short, kind phrases a parent could actually believe at 3 a.m.—something you remember the feeling of, not just the words.

Constraints

What forced good decisions.

Postpartum care isn't just code—it's triage for the nervous system. In a weekend, we had to move quickly without cutting corners on safety or trust, so every decision was weighed against one moment: a parent, awake in the dark, needing help now.

⏳ 36-hour deadline

To ship live telephony, AI, memory, and resources—safely. WhatsApp, dashboards, and semantic embeddings were cut so we could demo sub-5 second replies focused on what parents actually need.

🎯 Safety over scope

Information-only meant Auntie never freestyled medical advice. Every output had to fall into Comfort · Resource · Escalate, with deterministic handoffs.

🔒 Privacy by default

Short-lived context, minimal fields (delivery, feeding, weeks postpartum, region). We remembered only what helped—e.g. C-section recovery—and nothing more. Hashed phone numbers, retention documented.

⚖️ Judge-proof demo

Visible logs showing triage JSON, Atlas collections, and Snowflake queries live. Credibility came from receipts, not vibes.

Constraints didn't slow us—they drew the line between helpful and harmful. They forced a single mandate: deliver a humane, resilient line that comforts first, informs clearly, and escalates deterministically when risk appears.

Design Decisions

Auntie isn't a UI. She's a voice.

We treated "Auntie" as a persona with safety rails: warm and concise for SMS; fuller for voice; always evidence-grounded; never diagnostic.

Comfort:
"Baby hasn't latched in 3 hours—normal?"
Auntie: "You're not alone. Skin-to-skin can help. If no latch in 4–6 hours, call your provider. Two lactation lines near you: [links]."
Escalate:
"My 6-week-old has a fever of 38°C."
Auntie Voice: "Love, with a baby this young, that can be serious. Let's head to the nearest emergency department now. I'm sending the address. You're not alone."
We sketched dozens of micro-flows (latch basics, C-section pain, baby blues vs red flags, sleep triage), then cut to the flows a tired parent could use one-handed.
Must-haves

The non-negotiables

Texts are one-breath, kind, and end with a single next step. Calls are warmer and slower, with human pauses and plain language—tuned to sound like a real aunt, not a bot reading a script.

When risk appears, the tone stays steady: no sirens, no blame—just a gentle, actionable handoff. One Auntie. Two surfaces. No uncanny costume changes.

☎️ Phone-first distribution

Distribution is the product. SMS shipped first for reliability and speed; voice layered on when stable. No dashboards, no portals—just the lowest-friction path to comfort and care.

😌 One-breath messaging

Every SMS/call: a caring line to lower panic, one clear action, one "what to watch." If a message took two breaths to read, it got cut.

⚠️ Deterministic answers, not vibes

Gemini returns a structured intent—Comfort, Resource, or Escalate—plus topic and region. From there: compose the reply, attach 2–3 vetted resources. No black-box rummaging, no link dumps.

🔁 Safety contracts

Short-lived context (delivery type, feeding, weeks postpartum, region) tied to a hashed number. Enough to avoid repeating yourself. Not enough to feel like surveillance.

What We Built

We didn't prototype screens. We prototyped conversations.

The identity system

Instead of a visual design system, our core identity is how Auntie speaks and escalates. We defined:

→ Intent taxonomy: Comfort · Resource · Escalate
→ Evidence micro-cards: short, vetted snippets (WHO/CDC/ACOG/PSI) fed deterministically to ground answers
→ Red-flag lexicon + scripts: gentle, non-alarmist escalation language

SMS Copy Testing: Early drafts were too wordy—a tired parent skimmed and missed the action. We cut to "one breath" rules: one line of comfort, one step, one "what to watch."

Voice Tuning: OpenAI's TTS gave warmth, but some voices sounded uncanny. We iterated until Auntie sounded human, not robotic.

Escalation Language: First drafts felt clinical ("seek medical attention"). We softened: "Love, let's call your provider now—here's the number." The moms we worked with confirmed: balanced safety and calm. These weren't polish—they were product design.

Contracts:
Brain → Telephony returns { intent, topic, region, reply_text, red_flags }
Telephony ↔ Snowflake: topic + region → top 2–3 vetted resources
Telephony ↔ Mongo: short-lived session + minimal context for personalization
Clear contracts between Brain ↔ Telephony ↔ Snowflake ↔ Mongo let four people ship like one mind. Crucial for a 36-hour window.

Prototyping & iteration

By Sunday, two shippable prototypes that felt like the same Auntie across channels: SMS for fastest, lowest-friction support; Voice for hands-free reassurance when you're holding a baby at 3 a.m.

The SMS flow uses the "one-breath" rule: caring line → clear step → "what to watch." Grounded by Gemini's structured intent, with 2–3 vetted region-aware resources attached. After any call, Auntie follows up by text so guidance isn't lost in the moment.

The voice line uses the same persona, sized for audio: warmer pacing, natural pauses, same escalation copy. If risk appears: gentle, specific handoff. Post-call, Auntie texts the key steps so a sleep-deprived parent doesn't have to remember.

What we shipped — and why it mattered

In 36 hours we turned a fragile idea into a working lifeline: SMS first, voice optional, same warm Auntie across both. That mattered because our users aren't looking for a new app at 3 a.m.—they're looking for a human-sounding helper who answers in seconds and gives one doable next step. We proved that with live call + text flows on stage, letting a judge dial in and see Auntie's gentle reassurance, concise guidance, and the follow-up text so nothing gets lost.

Behind the warmth, the system held up: structured intent triage, privacy-first "light memory," and deterministic local resources—clear contracts between services instead of ad-hoc glue. That made the demo reliable, the handoffs safe, and the story credible to both judges and clinicians in the room.

What we're proud of

→ Multi-modal in a weekend. Live SMS + voice with natural TTS—same persona, tuned per channel—so parents could choose the lowest-friction path in the moment.

→ Triaging that feels kind, not clinical. Comfort/Resource/Escalate showed up in the copy, the routing, and the escalation language ("Love…")—a safety feature disguised as empathy.

→ Privacy-first memory. Short-lived context so Auntie remembers what matters without hoarding more than she needs.

→ Deterministic resources. Topic + region → 2–3 vetted links you can actually call—no model hand-waving.

→ Judge-proof artifacts. We showed the triage JSON, Atlas collections, and Snowflake lookups—receipts that the thing worked, not just vibes.

What we learned (and will keep)

→ Scope like surgeons. Tier-1 tags/micro-RAG beat "let's vectorize the world." Faster answers, easier to reason about under pressure.

→ Conversation design is product design. One-breath messages, tone guides, and red-flag scripts did more for trust than any UI flourish.

→ Contracts prevent meltdowns. Clear inputs/outputs between Brain ↔ Telephony ↔ Snowflake ↔ Mongo let four people ship like one mind.

→ Safety is a feature, not a disclaimer. Escalation copy that sounds like Auntie—warm, specific, actionable—turns a scary moment into a guided handoff.

Why we think we won

We designed for the real 3 a.m.—no apps, no accounts, no homework—and we proved it live. Judges didn't just hear a story; they called, texted, and watched Auntie route help. That blend of empathy, reliability, and receipts made the impact legible: tech that reduces cognitive load for exhausted new parents, with safety you can trust.

We wired the sponsor stack wall-to-wall: Gemini (intent + safety), MongoDB Atlas (short-lived memory), Snowflake API (deterministic, region-aware resources), Twilio (SMS/Voice), and OpenAI Realtime voice. All orchestrated in TypeScript/Node and deployed live. While most teams built an app, we shipped the channel parents already use at 3 a.m. and proved it on stage with real flows, visible triage JSON, and sub-5s SMS round-trip. That's what earned us a spot among the winners—and it's the bar we'll keep meeting.

Section 1 // Project Overview

New parents don’t quit because they’re “unmotivated.” They quit because the system makes help feel like homework. In the fourth trimester, energy and attention are the scarcest resources—precisely when most “solutions” demand the most: download an app, create an account, complete a 20-question intake, then wait.

My Auntie reframes support around the reality of 3 a.m.: a crying baby, 4% battery, and a brain made of mashed potatoes. No apps, no dashboards—just a warm voice or a short text that meets you where you are, offers one gentle next step, and (when needed) routes you to real care without judgment.

Our goal for the 36-hour sprint: ship a live, phone-first safety-aware companion that feels like calling a trusted auntie—tiny, kind, and doable. We launched SMS + voice, intent triage (Comfort · Resource · Escalate), privacy-first memory, and deterministic resource lookup—end-to-end and demo-ready.

Section 2 // The Challenge

Postpartum depression and anxiety affect ~1 in 7 women; many never receive timely care. Stigma (“I should be happy”), sleep fragmentation, and logistical friction push parents away from support at the exact moment they need it.

The dominant pattern in digital mental health—apps that shift the burden back to the parent (log in, track, remember)—is backwards at 3 a.m. We needed a low-friction, high-comfort channel that lowers cognitive load, is safe by design, and can escalate appropriately.

Reframe: Don’t build another mental-health app. Build what new parents actually use at 3 a.m.: the phone. A kind voice. A short text. A connector that knows when to comfort, when to send resources, and when to escalate.

You don’t want an app. You want your Auntie. Information-only this weekend; deterministic escalation; short-lived context for safety.

Section 3.1 // Let's set the scene

At 3 a.m. a new parent asks: “Is this normal?”

They don’t need a portal, they need a person. Real aunties aren’t always awake, and advice can be dated or risky. My Auntie keeps the warmth and removes the guesswork: zero setup, human-sounding support, and vetted resources when needed.

Two design constraints guided everything:
1. Cognitive load must be near zero. One-breath messages, one next step.
2. Safety must be explicit, not implied. Intent classification and clear red-flag routes to urgent care—without alarmism

This created an interesting opportunity, Jaguar has heritage, but it's not owning a lane. Unlike competitors, its identity isn't clear or confidently positioned.

Section 3.3 // Our Constraints

Postpartum care isn’t just code—it’s triage for the nervous system. In a weekend, we had to move quickly without cutting corners on safety or trust, so every decision was weighed against one moment: a parent, awake in the dark, needing help now.

Constraints didn’t slow us; they drew the line between helpful and harmful. They forced a single mandate: deliver a humane, resilient line that comforts first, informs clearly, and escalates deterministically when risk appears.

The Problem & Our Scope

We scoped to what mattered most in a weekend: a reliable SMS core, optional voice call, Gemini-based triage returning structured intent (Comfort/Resource/Escalate), a Mongo Atlas memory scaffold, and Snowflake resource matching by topic+region. Everything else—WhatsApp, embeddings, dashboards—comes later.

Section 5 // Design Process

5.1 // Identity System Development

Instead of a visual design system, our core identity is how Auntie speaks and escalates. We defined:

  • Intent taxonomy: Comfort · Resource · Escalate
  • Evidence micro-cards: short, vetted snippets (WHO/CDC/ACOG/PSI) fed deterministically to ground answers
  • Red-flag lexicon + scripts: gentle, non-alarmist escalation language

    • Contracts:
    Brain → Telephony returns { intent, topic, region, reply_text, red_flags }
    Telephony ↔ Snowflake: topic + region → top 2–3 vetted resources
    Telephony ↔ Mongo: short-lived session + minimal context for personalization on message #2
    The result is an “auntie” who feels human yet stays within safe, constrained rails—and a stack where no teammate needs to reach into another’s internals.

    5.2 // Prototyping & Iteration

    After defining our core features, we transitioned from paper sketches to interactive digital prototypes for both mobile and desktop. This dual-platform approach let us test how seniors engaged with the experience in different contexts — from tablets at home to full-screen interfaces at community centers.

    Each prototype underwent multiple rounds of usability testing, with direct feedback shaping refinements like clearer iconography, larger tap targets, and simplified flows. We prioritized accessibility every step of the way to ensure confidence, not confusion, at every tap or click.

    👩‍🍼️ What we shipped— and why it mattered

    In 36 hours we turned a fragile idea into a working lifeline: SMS first, voice optional, same warm Auntie across both. That mattered because our users aren’t looking for a new app at 3 a.m.—they’re looking for a human-sounding helper who answers in seconds and gives one doable next step. We proved that with live call + text flows on stage, letting a judge dial in and see Auntie’s gentle reassurance, concise guidance, and the follow-up text so nothing gets lost.

    Behind the warmth, the system held up: structured intent triage (Comfort · Resource · Escalate), privacy-first “light memory,” and deterministic local resources—clear contracts between services instead of ad-hoc glue. That made the demo reliable, the handoffs safe, and the story credible to both judges and clinicians in the room.

    🍼 What we're proud of

    → Multi-modal in a weekend. Live SMS + (optional) voice with natural TTS—same persona, tuned per channel—so parents could choose the lowest-friction path in the moment.

    → Triaging that feels kind, not clinical. Comfort/Resource/Escalate showed up in the copy, the routing, and the escalation language (“Love…”)—a safety feature disguised as empathy.

    → Privacy-first memory. Short-lived context (delivery, feeding, region) so Auntie remembers what matters without hoarding more than she needs.

    → Deterministic resources. Topic + region → 2–3 vetted links you can actually call—no model hand-waving.

    → Judge-proof artifacts. We showed the triage JSON, Atlas collections, and Snowflake lookups—receipts that the thing worked, not just vibes.

    🚼 What we learned (and will keep)

    → Scope like surgeons. Tier-1 (tags/micro-RAG) beat “let’s vectorize the world.” It made answers faster and the system easier to reason about under pressure.

    → Conversation design is product design. One-breath messages, tone guides, and red-flag scripts did more for trust than any UI flourish.

    → Contracts prevent meltdowns. Clear inputs/outputs between Brain ↔ Telephony ↔ Snowflake ↔ Mongo let four people ship like one mind.

    → Safety is a feature, not a disclaimer. Escalation copy that sounds like Auntie—warm, specific, actionable—turns a scary moment into a guided handoff.

    🤰 Why we think we won

    We designed for the real 3 a.m.—no apps, no accounts, no homework—and we proved it live. Judges didn’t just hear a story; they called, texted, and watched Auntie route help. That blend of empathy, reliability, and receipts made the impact legible: tech that reduces cognitive load for exhausted new parents, with safety you can trust. That’s what earned us a spot among the hackathon winners—and it’s the bar we’ll keep meeting as we expand regions, languages, and clinical review.

    And we did it in 36 hours. Not a slideware promise, a working service. We wired the sponsor stack wall-to-wall—Twilio (SMS/Voice), OpenAI Realtime (TTS for calls), Gemini (intent + safety), MongoDB Atlas (short-lived memory), Snowflake (deterministic, region-aware resources), all orchestrated in TypeScript/Node and deployed live. While most teams built “an app”, we shipped the channel parents already use at 3 a.m. —call and text—then proved it on stage with real flows, visible triage JSON, and sub-5s SMS round-trip. That combination of empathy + reliability + receipts is what made Auntie legible to judges as more than a demo—and why we landed among the winners.

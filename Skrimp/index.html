The Product

Skrimp collapses the grocery paradox. Deals, meals, and a shopping list — in one workflow.

People want complete control over their spend and to love what they're eating — but today that's three disconnected apps and a lot of guesswork. Skrimp reads local flyers, prices ingredients, and assembles budget-aware recipes into a living grocery list that adapts as you shop.

Not another dashboard to babysit — a calm, opinionated sous-chef that saves money without the Sunday spreadsheet marathon. The outcome is simple: meals your family will actually eat, at prices that finally work.

The Context

Food inflation hit 40-year highs. A family of four in Canada now spends over $16,000 a year.

Up ~28.6% from 2020. Younger households, already carrying 30% less wealth than boomers at the same age, feel the squeeze most. This isn't only economics; it's exhaustion. Parents describe "Sunday night planning" as triage: deals that don't match recipes, recipes that blow the budget, lists that don't survive the aisle.

The Problem & Our Scope

We weren't building a meal kit or a premium subscription. Skrimp is the connective tissue — unifying sales, meals, and budgets into one workflow that feels obvious once you use it.

We set ethical guardrails from day one: collect the minimum data required for utility, provide plain-language explanations for pricing and swaps, and design for consent and off-ramps (edit, delete, opt-out).

We reviewed recipes and defaults for cultural bias, created allergen filters and pantry-aware swaps, and avoided "one perfect plan" claims. The goal is control, not coercion.

Ideation

Every flow had to answer one question: does this reduce the user's cognitive load?

We explored flows that start with the budget, not the recipe. Early maps sequenced: postal code → store mix → dietary constraints → deal-aligned meal sets → dynamic list that adapts as prices change.

If a step felt like homework, we cut it or automated it. The insight from listening sessions was that users struggle to articulate what they want — but they know exactly what's making them struggle.

Budget-first engine: plans are generated to hit a target weekly spend, not retrofitted after the fact.
Calm UX: defaults do the heavy lifting; power users can still tweak stores, brands, and swaps.

Must-haves

Designing with sustainability and longevity in mind.

🧭 Price-linked recipes

Every meal ties to real local prices, and we show the user where the verifiable savings come from.

🔁 Pantry-aware swaps

Swap one ingredient, update total instantly; suggest common substitutes and use-up ideas.

🛡️ Ethical guardrails

Minimal data, clear consent, accessible explanations for "why we need this info" to ensure maximum savings while maintaining privacy.

♿ Accessibility

High contrast, large targets, predictable flows, lists organised by store layout; stress goes down as savings go up.

Design Process

From listening sessions to a live product — and an ongoing conversation with users.

5.1 // Meal Engine & Data Pipelines

We built a price-aware meal engine on top of flexible flyer ingestion. It normalises prices, maps them to ingredients, and composes meal sets that respect budget, time, and taste.

The system favours explainability: each plan shows why it's cheaper (e.g., "pasta + chickpeas are $0.XX/serving this week") and offers one-tap swaps with updated totals.

Explainable savings — every choice surfaces a simple reason and its dollar impact.
Resilient ingestion — handles layout changes, missing SKUs, and store quirks without breaking the plan.

[Photo: Heather Senoran / CTV News Kitchener, Sept 2025]

5.2 // Prototyping & Iteration

We prototyped mobile-first for real-world aisle use and desktop for planning at home. Usability passes tightened the loop between budget target → plan preview → cart/list.

Testing fast with parents and students surfaced key refinements: clearer per-meal totals, a "swap & recalc" pattern, and allergen-safe presets that persist between weeks.

Budget clarity — per-meal and per-week totals, with auto-recalc on every change.
Fewer taps — defaults that feel right; explicit switches for stores, brands, and dietary needs.
Ethical AI in practice — minimum data stored, local first where possible, easy edit/delete/export, and no dark patterns.

Section 1 // Project Overview

Skrimp collapses the grocery paradox. People want complete control over their spend and to love what they’re eating — but today that’s three disconnected apps and a lot of guesswork.

Skrimp reads local flyers, prices ingredients, and assembles budget-aware recipes into a living grocery list that adapts as you shop. Not another dashboard to babysit — a calm, opinionated sous-chef that saves money without the Sunday spreadsheet marathon.

The outcome is simple: meals your family will actually eat, at prices that finally work.

Section 2 // The Challenge

The grocery problem is not just money — it’s mental bandwidth.

→ Flyer apps show deals but no meal context.
→ Recipe apps look pretty but ignore cost.
→ Budget apps tally receipts after it’s too late.

Most families stop trying to coordinate all three and wing it in the aisle. The result is predictable: wasted time, wasted food, wasted cash.

Skrimp removes the coordination tax. You stay in control of the budget and the plate — without juggling tabs, coupons, and mental math.

Section 3.1 // Let's set the scene

Food inflation hit 40-year highs.

A family of four in Canada spends over $16,000 annually— up ~28.6% from 2020. Younger households, already carrying 30% less wealth than boomers at the same age, feel the squeeze most.

This isn’t only economics; it’s exhaustion. Parents describe “Sunday night planning” as triage: deals that don’t match recipes, recipes that blow the budget, lists that don’t survive the aisle.

The “grocery problem” is invisible in plain sight: everyone’s hacking it alone, but nobody has solved it end-to-end at scale.
Don’t add another app. Integrate the journey — deals → meals → list — and make savings feel like calm control.

Section 3.3 // Our Constraints

A tight scope —and families in immediate need— shaped the product:

Our constraints weren't obstacles — they were guardrails. They helped us focus the work around what mattered most: building a brand system that could evolve without losing its edge.

⏰ Early validation deadline

Velocity Cornerstone and city grant programs demanded fast traction with real users.

📊 Fragmented data sources

Flyer APIs and layouts vary wildly; Skrimp had to adapt without brittle integrations.

👤 Solo founder bandwidth

Feature creep was the enemy— every build had to prove immediate value to keep shipping things people want, and fast.

💸 User sensitivity

Families under cost stress wouldn’t pay upfront; Skrimp had to earn loyalty first.

The Problem & Our Scope

We weren’t building a meal kit or a premium subscription. Skrimp is the connective tissue — unifying sales, meals, and budgets into one workflow that feels obvious once you use it.

We set ethical guardrails from day one: collect the minimum data required for utility, provide plain-language explanations for pricing and swaps, and design for consent and off-ramps (edit, delete, opt-out).

We reviewed recipes and defaults for cultural bias, created allergen filters and pantry-aware swaps, and avoided “one perfect plan” claims. The goal is control, not coercion.

Section 4 // Ideating

We explored flows that start with the budget, not the recipe. Early maps sequenced: zip/postal-code → store mix → dietary constraints → deal-aligned meal sets → dynamic list that adapts as prices change.

Every direction answered one question: “Does this reduce the user’s cognitive load?” If a step felt like homework, we cut it or automated it.

Section 5 // Design Process

5.1 // Meal Engine & Data Pipelines

We built a price-aware meal engine on top of flexible flyer ingestion. It normalizes prices, maps them to ingredients, and composes meal sets that respect budget, time, and taste.

The system favors explainability: each plan shows why it’s cheaper (e.g., “pasta + chickpeas are $0.XX/serving this week”) and offers one-tap swaps with updated totals.

Explainable savings — Every choice surfaces a simple reason and its dollar impact.
Resilient ingestion — Handles layout changes, missing SKUs/info, and store quirks without breaking the plan.

5.2 // Prototyping & Iteration

We prototyped mobile-first for real-world aisle use and desktop for planning at home. Usability passes tightened the loop between budget target → plan preview → cart/list.

Testing fast with parents and students surfaced key refinements: clearer per-meal totals, a “swap & recalc” pattern, and allergen-safe presets that persist between weeks.

Budget clarity — Per-meal and per-week totals, with auto-recalc on every change.
Fewer taps — Defaults that feel right; explicit switches for stores, brands, and dietary needs.
Ethical AI in practice — Minimum data stored, local first where possible, easy edit/delete/export, and no dark patterns.
